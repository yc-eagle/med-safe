## 1. Read this before filming: one interview covers one user, not two

The product has two users. **They do not speak the same language**, so a single interview cannot carry both conclusions.

| User | Language | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Older adults in Hong Kong | **Cantonese** | Interview 1 |
| Foreign domestic helpers (mainly Filipina) | **English or Tagalog** — **not Cantonese** | Interview 2 |

**Tonight's confirmed plan is one Cantonese-speaking interviewee** (a Tsinghua student from Macau, with Cantonese-speaking family background).

**Therefore, unless a second English- or Tagalog-speaking interviewee is also filmed, the pitch must not present evidence about foreign domestic helpers.** Doing so is over-claiming, and one question from a judge exposes it.

### A note on the variety of Cantonese

The interviewee's background is **Macau Cantonese**, which is a Cantonese variety but not identical to Hong Kong Cantonese in vocabulary and usage. This is fine for the purpose — it demonstrates the **family caregiver** experience in a Cantonese-speaking household — but do not present the interviewee as "a Hong Kong local", and do not claim the clip is evidence about Hong Kong specifically beyond what they actually describe.

**Say it accurately:** "We interviewed a Cantonese-speaking family caregiver." Not: "We interviewed a Hong Kong local."

## 2. What to do if the English interview does not happen

**Do not fake it, and do not stretch the Cantonese clip.** Choose one of these instead:

**Option A — narrow the claim (recommended, zero extra work)**

Change the slide from "evidence about both users" to "evidence about the family caregiver", and support the foreign domestic helper user **separately, from documented sources** rather than from an interview:

- The helper is the person who physically handles the pills and prompts the doses
- She cannot read Chinese labels
- The product's speech output exists because of her

This is honest, it still lands, and it requires no new footage.

**Option B — add a second clip.** If an English- or Tagalog-speaking interviewee becomes available, record the same questions. This is the stronger version and restores the two-clip slide.

**Option C — bring Ella in on camera.** Ella is a clinician. A 30-second segment where she explains why "the caregiver cannot read the label" is a real medication-safety problem is a **different kind of evidence** (expert framing, not user evidence) — useful, but label it as that. Do not present expert opinion as user evidence.

## 3. The seven questions

**How this works.** The team does not speak Cantonese. The interviewer therefore asks **in Mandarin**, reading the simplified-Chinese wording below, and **the interviewee answers in Cantonese**. That is an everyday situation and it is fine, but it creates four disciplines you must follow — they are set out after the table.

**Read the questions exactly as written.** They are deliberately phrased as questions about a **specific remembered occasion** rather than a habit or an opinion. "What happened the last time" produces a usable account; "what do you usually do" produces a generalisation.

**Ask these seven. Do not add a survey.** The value is in the unguarded answer, not in coverage.

| # | Question to read aloud | What it is there to get |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 你或者你家里长辈，每天大概要吃几种药？ | Establishes polypharmacy |
| 2 | 最近一次你不确定某个药该怎么吃，是什么情况？当时你怎么处理的？ | A concrete incident, not a general statement |
| 3 | 医生讲完之后，你当时有什么想问但没问出口的吗？ | Information is lost at handover |
| 4 | 药盒里那张纸，你上一次打开看是什么时候？看得懂吗？ | Whether the leaflet is actually used |
| 5 | 最近一次你要同时吃两种药的时候，你有没有先去查它们能不能一起吃？你是怎么查的？ | The core question: nobody performs this check |
| 6 | 家里有人帮忙照顾吗？她是靠什么知道哪盒药是什么的？ | Leads to the helper user. Note this is testimony **about** a helper, not the helper's own account |
| 7 | 如果有一样东西，拍一下就能告诉你"这两盒不能一起吃"，你会用吗？ | Validates the need |

**If a second interviewee speaking English or Tagalog becomes available**, ask questions 1-5 in English, and replace questions 5 and 6 with:

- "How do you know which pill is which, when the label is in Chinese?"
- "If you are not sure whether two medicines can be taken together, who do you ask?"

### Four disciplines created by the Mandarin-question / Cantonese-answer setup

**1. Cut the question audio in the edit and use on-screen text instead.**
The pitching round is in English. A Mandarin question cutting into a Cantonese conversation is jarring, and it makes the interviewee look like a Hong Kong local when they are not. **Do this: on-screen English question, then the Cantonese answer, then hard English subtitles.** Keep the Mandarin question audio during editing so you can match each answer to its question.

**2. Question 4 must not be used to argue that leaflets omit interactions.**
Leaflets **do** contain interaction sections. This question is only there to find out whether the person actually reads and understands the leaflet. Do not build a claim on "nobody ever mentions it".

**3. The interviewee is in Macau, so the account describes Macau healthcare.**
Hong Kong and Macau differ in health system, drug register and label format. Therefore:

- You may say: "We interviewed a Cantonese-speaking family caregiver, and the difficulty she describes is real."
- You may **not** say: "This demonstrates the situation in Hong Kong."
- Do not use this clip to support any Hong Kong-specific claim, such as HK registration numbers or Hospital Authority follow-up procedures.

**4. State the honesty limit before someone else does.**
The interviewee is speaking about a relative, so this is second-hand testimony. It is legitimate as evidence **that the problem exists**. It must **not** be described as "first-hand research in Hong Kong". The accurate wording is "we interviewed a Cantonese-speaking family caregiver".

## 4. Follow-up prompts

When an answer comes back vague, **do not move to the next question — dig into this one.**

| They say | You ask |
|---|---|
| "I just take them, whatever" | "Which medicine was it the last time? Where were you?" |
| "It should be fine" | "How did you know it was fine?" |
| "I asked the doctor, I think" | "When was the last time? Were you clear afterwards?" |
| "My family handles that" | "How do they know which box is which?" |
| "I never thought about it" | Keep this answer. It is one of the most valuable kinds |

## 5. Filming discipline

- [ ] Film in **landscape**
- [ ] **Separate audio capture** (a second phone close to the subject)
- [ ] Shoot **3-5 minutes**, edit down to **60-90 seconds**
- [ ] **Keep the moments where the subject says "I don't know" or "I never thought about it"** — real is more credible than fluent
- [ ] **Keep the Mandarin question audio too** — you need it to match answers to questions while editing, even though it will be cut from the final clip
- [ ] Back up the audio **immediately** (phone plus cloud)
- [ ] **Complete `consent.md` before leaving** — consent cannot be collected afterwards
- [ ] **Ask permission explicitly for public use.** The clip will be shown on stage and published in a public repository
- [ ] Note on `consent.md` that the interviewee speaks Cantonese and the interviewer asked in Mandarin, so the edit is not misread later
